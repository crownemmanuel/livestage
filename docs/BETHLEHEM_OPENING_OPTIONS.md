# Opening into Bethlehem: options

Where it sits: the prologue film ends on the storybook Jacob at his bench ("…and they called him —" / kids:
"Joseph is home!"). Then the wall must become the real Bethlehem street (BG-01) at life size, as an extension of the
stage, and the show title SALVATION · THE HOUSE OF DAVID appears somewhere in the change. The stage is dark or
nearly dark during the change; actors enter once the street is in place.

Rule for all options: no push-ins. The finished street is always seen from audience eye height (about 1.7 m) at
true scale (see SCENE_BACKDROPS.md, "The wall is part of the set").

## Option 1: the set builds itself (big-budget Broadway set change)
1. The storybook Jacob fades as the parchment page lifts away like a scrim flying out.
2. Behind it: an empty stage of the real world: the packed-earth street, a big morning sky, the hills of Bethlehem
   far off (a painted backdrop flying in).
3. The title SALVATION · THE HOUSE OF DAVID writes itself in gold across the sky.
4. The set arrives piece by piece, each at full scale: house fronts truck in from the wings on both sides and lock
   into place with a soft thud, the upper rows of houses rise up from below like stage lifts, Jacob's workshop rolls
   in from the right, the palms fly in from above, doors swing shut, the oven's smoke starts, pigeons land.
5. The title fades; the lights come up on the finished street.
   Feels like a set being pushed into place, which is what you described. Easy to make in Blender (each piece is
   an animated object in the town we already have), then Seedance turns it photoreal.

## Option 2: the pop-up book
The storybook page with Jacob folds back flat, and the whole street pops up out of the page like a giant pop-up
book: walls fold up, roofs flip over, the paper turns to real stone as each piece stands up. The title is printed on
the page and rises with it. Strong link to the prologue's book; less "real theatre".

## Option 3: the revolve
Jacob's storybook workshop is the back of a house front. It turns around on a turntable (we see it rotate in
depth), and the front side is the real Jacob's house on the real street; the other houses turn in the same way,
one after another, like a row of revolving flats. Title in the sky while they turn.

## Option 4: the scrim and the dawn
Simplest and very theatrical: the storybook street is a painted gauze. As dawn light grows behind it, the painting
fades and the real street appears through it at the same place, same scale; then smoke, birds and people start
moving. Title fades up in the sky during the dawn.

## Recommendation
Option 1, starting with the scrim from option 4 (the parchment lifts away like a gauze), so the change reads as a
real set change by a theatre with a big budget, and ends on the full street at true scale.

## After the opening (rest of Scene 1, same rule)
- Arrival: the camera only tracks sideways at eye height to follow Joseph in from the left.
- The Workshop: no push-in. Lighting shift to late-afternoon gold on the same street (the workshop is already on
  the right), or the workshop truck slides to centre while the other houses slide off.
- Farewell: Joseph walks off right; the street stays; the map flies in over it (TR-02).
